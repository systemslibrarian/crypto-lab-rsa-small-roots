RSA Small Roots

Coppersmith · Howgrave-Graham · Exact LLL

Recover an unknown RSA message suffix or a prime from known high bits, and inspect every step from the lattice to the verified answer.

WHY IT MATTERS

A large message can hide a small secret. If enough of its structure is known, that small missing part can expose the whole message—even when ordinary cube roots fail.

THE IDEA BEHIND THE ATTACK

Too large for a cube root. Small enough for a lattice.

01

Try the shortcut

If the entire message is so small that cubing never wraps around the modulus, an integer cube root recovers it.

Preparing exact cube-root checks.

This is the integer cube-root attack. Håstad’s broadcast attack instead combines ciphertexts by CRT and compares with the product of the recipients’ moduli. Explore RSA Forge.

02

Keep just the unknown small

The prefix shifts a small suffix into a much larger message. Polynomial shifts encode the modular equation in an integer lattice.

Known prefix + small x
↓ scaled polynomial coefficients ↓
Integer lattice

The factor-hint scenario uses the same idea, but the equation vanishes modulo an unknown divisor. It needs leaked high bits and does not rely on a small public exponent.

03

Make modular become integer

LLL finds short combinations. When an integer is divisible by a large modulus and smaller in magnitude than that modulus, it must be zero.

bᵐ divides h(x₀)
|h(x₀)| < bᵐ
⇒ h(x₀) = 0 over the integers

Exact Sturm sequences isolate roots without scanning every suffix. Re-encryption or candidate divisibility supplies the final public check.

The mathematics, assumptions & sources

The basis and its bound

For degree r, multiplicity m, and t extra shifts, n = rm + t. Rows are N^(m−i) x^j f^i for 0 ≤ i < m, 0 ≤ j < r, followed by x^j f^m for 0 ≤ j < t. Column ℓ is scaled by X^ℓ. Completed rows are never reduced modulo N.

D = N^[rm(m+1)/2] X^[n(n−1)/2]. With δ = 3/4, the finite sufficient inequality is C X^Q < N^E, where Q = n(n−1), C = n^n 2^[n(n−1)/2], and E = 2βmn − rm(m+1). β = 1 for messages and 1/2 for the promised larger prime.

The sufficient bound is a guarantee for this construction, not a boundary beyond which recovery is impossible. Asymptotic scales N^(1/3) and N^(1/4) require suitable growing lattice parameters; they are not the finite guarantees for every browser preset.

The certificate

For the selected original vector v, S = Σvℓ². Require nS < N^(2m) for the message, or nS < N^m for the larger-prime hint. Cauchy–Schwarz then gives |h(x₀)| < b^m. Integer row operations preserve divisibility by b^m, so h(x₀) = 0. The factor certificate is conditional on the declared larger-prime promise until a factor is found.

What this teaches about RSA

Increasing e is not a general repair for textbook RSA’s lack of a secure encryption encoding. This known-prefix/small-suffix equation does not directly describe correctly implemented RSA-OAEP. Read PKCS #1, §7.1 for the actual OAEP construction.

Out of scope: Håstad implementation, Wiener, Boneh–Durfee, Blömer–May, arbitrary exponents or multivariate polynomials, known-LSB hints, ROCA, BKZ, and an OAEP attack.
